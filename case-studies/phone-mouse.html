Phone-as-Mouse

Your iPhone, but it's a mouse. Point the camera at any surface — a desk, a notebook, your jeans — and the optical flow from the camera feed becomes cursor motion on your laptop. No hardware, no Bluetooth setup, just open the app and start moving.

Role Designer · AI-assisted builder
Stack SwiftUI (iOS) · Python (Windows) · Claude Code · Cursor
Timeframe 2026 · in progress

↑ Optical flow → cursor pad → cursor pad gestures → custom cursor.

01 The idea

Your phone is already the best input device you own.

You carry it everywhere. It has a camera with computer-vision tracking baked in. Why are we still buying separate pieces of plastic to move a cursor? Phone-as-Mouse turns the camera into the sensor — point it at any textured surface and the optical flow algorithm reads the motion the way an optical mouse does, just from further away.

02 What's working

Four features in the prototype.

01 Optical flow tracking Point the rear camera at any surface — the cursor follows. Core feature, the whole reason the rest exists.
02 Cursor pad — touch mode For when the camera approach isn't practical. The phone screen becomes a trackpad, like a mini Magic Trackpad in your pocket.
03 Cursor pad — gestures Two-finger scroll, tap-to-click, drag — the small things that make a trackpad feel like a trackpad.
04 Custom cursor Pick a cursor that looks like you. Because if your input device is a screen, the cursor should match the vibe.
03 How I built it

iOS app, Windows companion, AI in the middle.

The iPhone runs a SwiftUI app that streams camera frames and touch events over the local network. A small Python service on Windows reads the stream and translates it into mouse movement. Both halves were scaffolded and iterated with Claude Code and Cursor — most of the typing was AI, most of the *what to build next* was me.

04 Where it's headed

Small device, big posture experiment.

The interesting question isn't "can a phone be a mouse" — it's "what does a desk feel like when the mouse is just whatever you happen to be holding." Working through this prototype is teaching me what people actually want from a pointing device, and what gets in the way.
