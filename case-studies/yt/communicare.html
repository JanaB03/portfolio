Shipped · Field work

CommuniCare

A device-agnostic communication platform for residents and case managers at Father Joe's Villages, San Diego.

RoleDesigner · UX engineer
Year2025
PartnerFather Joe's Villages — largest homeless services provider in San Diego

↑ CommuniCare home — designed for any device that walks in the door

01 Context

Designing where the users actually are.

CommuniCare had to work on any device that walks in the door: shared kiosks, donated Androids, a case manager's iPhone, an old laptop in the resource room. We spent time at Father Joe's. Sat in waiting rooms. Watched what "communication" actually looks like when you don't always have a phone, don't always have charge, and the people you need to reach are intermittent.

The biggest unmet need wasn't "how to send messages" — it was trust that the message got through. That reframed half the product.

02 Design

Lowest-common-denominator first.

Big touch targets. High contrast. No-account-required flows. Offline-friendly screens so the app doesn't break when the wifi blinks. Type sized for older users and low-light kiosks.

Tested every screen with actual residents. Every iteration came back with "this word means something else here" — language was a bigger design problem than layout.

03 Build

Tight loop with engineers and field staff.

Design decisions reviewed at the same table as build constraints. Case managers tested screens in their actual workflows; their feedback closed bugs the dev team would never have seen. Shipping required the field team's buy-in as much as the design team's polish.

04 Outcome

Shipped. In use across Father Joe's programs.

The humbling part was realizing "shipped" was a milestone, not the finish — every new resident reveals an edge case we hadn't designed for. Equal parts humbling, hard, and the most meaningful design work I've done.
