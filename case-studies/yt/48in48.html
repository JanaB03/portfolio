@janabridi TL;DR 1 year ago

48in48 pairs design + dev teams with nonprofits — 48 hours, build a real shipping website. Our team got UB Healthy Project, a food-equity nonprofit in San Diego that runs cooking classes for older adults.

We shipped, we won 1st of 50 teams, and the site is still live. The constraint was the gift.

♥ Hearted by @janabridi
01
@janabridi Discovery 1 year ago

Four hours to learn the whole organization.

The opening session was a stakeholder interview with the nonprofit's founder. Four hours to understand who they serve, what they need from a website right now, and what they wish they could do but can't afford. Took notes like crazy.

The brief got cut down on the spot — only the things that would make a difference on Monday morning.

02
@janabridi Design 1 year ago

Calm color palette. Big type. Older-user-first.

The volunteer base and audience skew older. So: oversized type, deep contrast, no decorative animation, big tap targets. Palette pulled from food — leafy greens, warm golden yellows. Friendly without being childish.

Tradeoff: skipped the slick hero animation everyone wanted in the kickoff. The site reads instead of performs.

03
@janabridi Build 1 year ago

Decisions made fast. Decisions stuck.

Cross-functional team — I led design while devs built in parallel. The hack: every design decision had a 10-minute fuse. If we couldn't agree in 10 minutes, the lead designer (me) called it and we moved on.

Zero revisits. No "what if we changed the nav at 3am". Velocity over optimization.

04
@janabridi Outcome 1 year ago

1st place. Site still live.

50 teams. We won. More importantly the nonprofit got a real website out of the weekend — not a deck, not a Figma file, an actual ubhealthyproject.org they can hand to grant-writers and program staff.

Best lesson of the year on what "design done" can mean when the deadline is hard.
