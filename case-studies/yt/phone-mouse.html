iPhone → desktop mouse via the camera. Four features in the prototype: optical-flow tracking, cursor pad touch mode, trackpad gestures, and a custom cursor. The clip above runs them in order.

iOS app talks to a tiny Python service on Windows. Most of the typing was AI. Most of the "what to build next" was me.

01
@janabridi Research 6 days ago

Your phone is the best input device you already own.

You carry it everywhere. It has a camera with computer-vision tracking baked in. Why are we still buying separate pieces of plastic to move a cursor? The project started as a "what if" — by hour two of prototyping it was clearly possible.

02
@janabridi Design 6 days ago

Two input modes, one mental model.

Designed two interaction modes — camera mode (point at a surface) and pad mode (touch the screen). Both produce cursor motion; the user's body chooses which one feels right. Kept the iPhone UI minimal so attention stays on the surface, not on the app.

The custom cursor was a late add — once the cursor felt like mine, the laptop felt different.

03
@janabridi Build 6 days ago

SwiftUI on iOS, Python on Windows, AI in the middle.

The iPhone runs a SwiftUI app that streams camera frames + touch events over the local network. A small Python service on Windows reads the stream and translates it into mouse movement.

  • Claude Code scaffolded the iOS app + the Windows service.
  • Cursor handled file-by-file refinements.
  • ChatGPT for prompt sharpening when I got stuck.
04
@janabridi Reflection 6 days ago

Small device, big posture experiment.

The interesting question isn't "can a phone be a mouse" — it's "what does a desk feel like when the mouse is just whatever you happen to be holding." Working through this prototype is teaching me what people actually want from a pointing device, and what gets in the way.
