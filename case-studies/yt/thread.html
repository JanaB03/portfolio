↑ A quick walkthrough — taking notes without leaving the scroll

01 The idea

Save isn't really save.

I noticed I was hoarding "saved" posts I'd never look at again. The save button feels like remembering, but it's really just deferring. Thread reframes the moment: a one-tap note slides in over the video, you jot the why — the recipe, the quote, the idea it sparked — and it lands in a real note you actually own.

No re-finding. No "wait which post was that." Just thoughts threaded to the thing that triggered them.

02 Design

The note shouldn't break the scroll.

The hardest design choice was where the note lives. A modal kills the scroll. A separate app means context-switching. So I designed it as a slim overlay that slides in over whatever you're watching — one tap, one field, dismiss to keep scrolling.

Type matters. The post was the trigger; the note has to feel secondary in hierarchy but fast in input.

03 Build

Designed by hand. Built by AI.

Every line of code came out of an AI tool. My job was the taste — the flow, the feel, what stays, what gets cut. The AI's job was the typing.

04 Reflection

The designer becomes the director.

Building Thread without writing code myself changed how I think about my own role. The bottleneck wasn't the typing — it was knowing what to make and what to leave out. AI tools collapse the gap between "I want it to feel like this" and "here it is, running."

The taste is still mine. The hands are faster.
