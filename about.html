The Story

How I got here

I grew up drawing — characters, rooms, little worlds. In college I drifted into cognitive science at UCSD because I wanted to know why interfaces frustrated my mom and felt invisible to my dad. That question kept following me.

It led to field research with Father Joe's Villages in San Diego, designing CommuniCare — a communication tool for people experiencing homelessness. To a 48-hour hackathon for a food-equity nonprofit (we shipped a full website and won first place out of 50 teams). To an EEG-based ADHD detector for my Brain-Computer Interfaces capstone. And now to Phone-as-Mouse, a tiny tool that turns an iPhone into a desktop mouse just by reading the camera.

The thread is the same: I want to build things that meet people where they are.

How I Work

Four things I keep close

  1. 01

    Research before pixels.

    I sit with the people first. Pixels are easy. Knowing who they're for is the hard part.

  2. 02

    Tinker fast, polish later.

    Twenty rough sketches beat one perfect mockup. Polish only what survives.

  3. 03

    Design and build.

    Closing that loop — from idea to shipped — is the whole job. I don't hand off, I follow through.

  4. 04

    Steal from cog sci.

    People aren't bugs to fix. The brain has rules, and good design bends with them.

"The details are not the details. They make the design."
